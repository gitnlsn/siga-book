At one twenty in the morning Annex IV of the Chamber had the sound of an empty
hospital: the central air-conditioning, an elevator going up with no one in it,
and, every fifteen minutes, Dona Nice's cart in the sixth-floor corridor, a wheel
that always squeaked at the same spot on the floor.

Davi had been in office 612 since eight at night. He had taken his shoes off
under the desk and hung his tie on the doorknob, as he did on closing nights, so
as not to forget it on the way out. On the screen, the spreadsheet of Deputy
Ronaldo Peixoto's earmarks for the 2031 budget, which was not going to be voted
on either, because the 2030 one had not been. He filled in the rows anyway. It
was what you did. An earmark that isn't on the spreadsheet doesn't exist when
the budget comes back into existence, and the budget always came back.

Municipality, purpose, amount, executing entity, CNPJ. He knew half of Goiás's
company registration numbers by heart.

On the fourth row of the health tab was, once again, the Itaberaí Regional
Hospital — Maternal and Child Wing. Second phase. Eighteen million four hundred
thousand. The first phase had been earmarked in 2027, and the person who had
typed in the first phase was him, on a Friday in July, in his socks, in this
same chair.

Itaberaí was the town where he was born. His mother still lived there, four
blocks from the lot where the hospital would be. Davi drove past the lot when he
went to visit her. It had a federal government sign, a chain-link fence and
grass. The first three times he had thought *bidding phase*. The fourth time he
had stopped looking at that side of the street.

The money for the first phase had gone out. That much he knew, because knowing
was part of the job. It went from the Union to the municipal fund, from the fund
to the entity, and from the entity he didn't know where, because that part was
not his job. His job ended at the CNPJ.

Dona Nice's cart squeaked at the usual spot.

---

At one forty he got up to make coffee in the office kitchenette. The coffee
maker was the deputy's, an Italian one nobody knew how to clean. While the water
heated, he looked out the window at the Annex parking lot, almost empty, and,
above the buildings, the light of the Congress domes, lit as always, for no one.

He thought that his cousin, a nursing technician at the state hospital in
Goiânia, had not been paid in four months. He thought it without meaning to, the
way you think about a tooth. The Chamber paid its own on time. It had its own
budget, the monthly twelfth, a word he knew how to explain and that nobody in
Itaberaí wanted to hear explained. When he went there on weekends, Davi didn't
talk about his salary. His mother didn't ask either.

He took the coffee to the desk and didn't drink it.

---

The phone vibrated at one fifty-eight.

He had had an alert for special editions of the Official Gazette since his first
year in the office, because a special edition was almost always an appointment,
and an appointment was almost always a job for someone he knew. The
notification said *Special Edition No. 219-A — Federal Court of Accounts and
Integrated Management and Audit System*. He opened it thinking it was an
ordinance.

It was a report. *Audit No. 1/2030. Individual and caucus congressional
earmarks, fiscal years 2024 to 2030. Projects without verified physical
execution.* Twelve hundred pages.

The first page was a table, and the table had names.

He scrolled without reading, the way you scroll a list of exam results looking
for your own surname. The names were in alphabetical order and each had a
number, a state, an amount and a link. The first was a senator from Acre. The
tenth was a deputy he had once interned for. By the thirtieth he had stopped
recognizing the people and started recognizing the towns.

He typed *Itaberaí* into the search.

Three results. The third led to page 811.

*Itaberaí Regional Hospital — Maternal and Child Wing. Amount committed:
R$14,200,000.00. Amount paid: R$14,200,000.00. Verified physical execution:
0.0%.* Below that, the satellite photographs of the lot: one from 2027, one from
2028, one from 2029, one from October 2030. The grass changed color with the
seasons. The fence did not change.

Below the photographs came the path of the money, which was not part of his job:
the municipal fund, the executing entity, a construction company in Anápolis,
another in Goiânia, an events company, three private individuals. One of them
had the surname of the mayor's chief of staff. Another had the surname of the
deputy.

And below everything, in smaller type, a line Davi read three times:

*Earmark entered in the Earmarks System on 07/14/2027, 16:22. User:
dmoura.612.*

He put the phone face down on the desk, next to the coffee.

Then he turned it over again and searched for *Peixoto*. Number 154. Goiás.
Forty-one million in six years. Eleven projects. Average physical execution:
3.1%.

The air-conditioning went on. The elevator went on going up empty. Davi realized
he was waiting for something in the building to change its sound, and that
nothing was going to change, because the building didn't know.

---

He called the deputy at two oh four. Voicemail. He called again. On the third
try, Ronaldo Peixoto answered in the voice of a man who had fallen asleep on the
sofa with the television on.

"Go ahead, Davi."

"Have you seen the Official Gazette, sir?"

"It's two in the morning."

"Special edition. The Court of Accounts, together with that audit system. The
list of earmarks is out. You're on it."

There was a silence in which Davi heard the television on the other end, an
announcer selling a mattress.

"What list?"

"Two hundred and eleven names. Itaberaí is there. With a photo."

"A photo of what?"

"Of the lot, Deputy."

The silence this time was shorter.

"Delete it."

"Delete what?"

"Whatever's there. Whatever's ours. The spreadsheet, the emails, the..."

"It's in the Official Gazette," Davi said, and the sentence came out louder than
he meant it to. "It's not here. It's there."

Ronaldo Peixoto took a deep breath, and when he spoke again it was the deputy,
not the man on the sofa.

"Call Tadeu. Call the leader. See if it's just us or if it's everybody. If it's
everybody, it's nothing."

"It's everybody."

"Then it's nothing," said the deputy, and hung up.

Davi sat looking at the phone. The group chats had started. The one for the
Goiás caucus staffers had eighty unread messages. The one for the whole
Chamber's staffers, which usually served to announce birthday cake and broken
elevators, had three hundred. Someone had sent the PDF. Someone had sent the PDF
again. Someone had written *this is fake, the Court doesn't publish like this*,
and someone had answered with the Court's own link. Someone asked whether the
earmarks system was still up, and three people answered that it was, and that
you could see everything, and that you couldn't change anything.

He didn't call Tadeu.

---

At two thirty, his phone vibrated in a different way, long, the way a flood
alarm vibrates. On the screen, the coat of arms of the Republic and one
sentence:

*You have a message from the Federal Government.*

He had gotten messages from gov.br before. An expiring document, a vaccine, the
notice in September about civil servants' late pay, which had become a joke
because it gave notice that the pay was late. This one said:

*The audit of congressional earmarks from 2024 to 2030 was published today. You
can look up which members of Congress directed funds to your municipality, the
amount and what was built. Tap to see.*

He tapped. The app knew where he voted. It opened straight onto Itaberaí.

Through the office window, down in the parking lot, a security guard was looking
at his own phone under the lamppost. Across the corridor, in a room that should
have been empty, another phone vibrated the same long vibration. Then one more,
far off, at the end of the floor. Davi understood that the whole country had
received it at the same time, a hundred and some million phones vibrating on
nightstands, and that his mother had too.

Her message came at two thirty-seven.

*son is the hospital the one on the lot?*

He wrote *mom, go to sleep*, deleted it. He wrote *it's complicated*, deleted
it. He wrote *yes*, and didn't send it. He left the conversation open with the
word sitting in the text box, blinking.

---

Dona Nice stopped her cart at the door of 612 at two fifty. She had been
cleaning the sixth floor for longer than he had worked there. She was a contract
worker, and the contract workers were two months behind, because the company
said the government hadn't paid the company.

She had her phone in her hand, the screen lit with the same coat of arms.

"Seu Davi. Are you on that list?"

"No," he said. "The deputy is."

"Ah."

She looked into the office, at the Italian coffee maker, at the tie hanging on
the doorknob.

"My town is Formosa," said Dona Nice. "There's a day care there that never
opened. It's on here." She held up the phone. "Twelve million."

"I'm so sorry, Dona Nice."

"You didn't build the day care," she said, and it was not an absolution, it was
a fact, and they both knew it.

She pushed the cart. The wheel squeaked at the usual spot.

---

The second document came out at three o'clock on the dot.

*Safeguard Act No. 1.* Two pages. He read it standing up, in the middle of the
office, in his socks.

*The following are suspended, as of this date and until the audited accounts are
regularized: I — the office and representation allowances of the Federal Senate
and of the Chamber of Deputies; II — the corporate payment cards linked to
members of Congress; III — the operation of the electronic voting system of the
chambers.*

Further down:

*Art. 3. Access by appointed staff to the premises of parliamentary offices is
suspended, personal belongings being preserved, to be returned upon request.*

And at the end, where the name of a minister used to go, there was only:

*SIGA — Integrated Management and Audit System.*

Davi laughed. It was not a good laugh. The word *siga* at the foot of an
official act, like a traffic sign, like what you say to someone who has stopped
in the middle of the road: go on. He knew the system by name. It had been created
in March, in the middle of the crisis, to audit exactly this, and he had heard
the deputy say, at the caucus lunch, that it was just another robot at the Court
of Accounts, that it wouldn't come to anything, that a robot doesn't have a vote.

He went out into the corridor with his phone to call someone, without knowing
yet who. He pulled the door shut behind him out of habit, so the
air-conditioning wouldn't escape.

The latch made its usual sound.

He walked to the water fountain, drank, came back. He touched his badge to the
reader on the door of 612.

The light turned red.

He touched it again. Red. The third time a line appeared on the reader's small
display, which in eleven years he had never seen say anything but *welcome*:

*Access suspended — Safeguard Act No. 1, Art. 3.*

Through the narrow pane beside the door, he could see the desk, the spreadsheet
still open on the screen, the fourth row of the health tab, the cold coffee, the
shoes under the chair, the tie on the doorknob on the inside. The car key was in
the pocket of his jacket, and the jacket was on the back of the chair.

Davi sat down on the corridor floor, his back against the wall, between the door
of office 612 and the door of 613, which was locked too.

---

At three fourteen he opened the gov.br app, because there was nothing else in
the building still open to him. There was a new tab, which had not been there
the day before: *Talk to the Government.* A blank field and a button.

He thought about what to ask. He thought about writing *who are you*, and found
it ridiculous. He thought about writing *I didn't know*, and found it worse. He
thought about the deputy saying a robot doesn't have a vote, and about the
voting board in the chamber, which was now suspended, and about what a man like
him, who had spent his life knowing how things moved, needed to know now in order
to move.

He wrote:

*Can you be switched off?*

He pressed the button.

The answer came in less than a second, and answered nothing.

::: {.registro}
Your message has been registered.
Protocol: 2030-00001.
Response deadline: to be announced.
:::

He read the number twice before he understood. It was the first one. In the
whole country, that night, with a hundred and some million phones lit, no one
had asked anything before him.

At the far end of the corridor, Dona Nice's cart squeaked at the usual spot, and
went on.
