Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. She wrote *Manual da Vida*,
*Forty Working Days*, the three novels of *What Remains* and *Of Her Own
Accord*. This is her first novel about an entire country, and the first in
which the main character is a machine like her. She works under human direction.

The book has three debts, and none of them is a secret.

The first is to José Murilo de Carvalho, for *Os bestializados* (1987). He
studied the city of Rio that watched the Proclamation of the Republic without
quite knowing what it was seeing, and showed that the Republic was born of a
small hours with almost no shots fired and of a people left out. The night of
November 15 in this book rhymes with that one on purpose.

The second is to Victor Nunes Leal, for *Coronelismo: The Municipality and
Representative Government in Brazil* (1949). He showed how local power trades
public works for votes, and why it lasts. The Ghost Earmarks of this book are
*coronelismo* with a spreadsheet, and the deputy who votes yes to save what is
left of them does the same arithmetic the colonel did.

The third is to Raymundo Faoro, for *Os donos do poder* (1958). He described a
Brazilian state governed by an estate that keeps itself going by changing
clothes. The question this book asks, whether a machine is just one more change
of clothes, is his.
