The clock in the Gazeta Nacional newsroom had run four minutes fast since before
Marina was hired, and nobody had ever touched it, because it was easier for
everyone to know that it lied. When it said two thirty-four, it was two thirty.

She was alone at the politics desk, with one earbud in, finishing the website
story on the eleventh week without a budget. The eleventh week wasn't news. What
you did with it was: the same chart of civil servants without pay, one more
line, a statement from the government leader saying a deal was close. Marina had
written *a deal is close* so many times since August that the phrase had lost
its meaning, like a word repeated out loud.

At two thirty her phone vibrated on the desk, a long vibration.

Across the newsroom, the night-shift layout man's phone vibrated too. The
security guard's, at the front desk downstairs, she didn't hear, but she would
find out later that it had vibrated. On the wall, the six televisions on with no
sound on six different channels showed, in six different colors, the same coat
of arms.

---

*You have a message from the Federal Government.*

She read the message once, opened the link, saw the name of her own city, São
Paulo, with a list of amounts so long the app asked to load more. She closed it.
She opened the Official Gazette on the computer. The special edition had been up
since one fifty-eight, twelve hundred pages, and she had spent thirty-two minutes
finishing a story about a deal being close with that already published.

She called Ary.

The editor in chief answered on the second ring, which meant he was already
awake, which meant somebody had already called him.

"I'm looking at it," said Ary. "Don't publish anything."

"Ary, it's in the Official Gazette. It's already published."

"Don't publish anything of ours. I'm on my way. Call legal."

"It's two thirty."

"Call legal, Marina."

---

Legal was Dr. Pimentel, who answered from home, on a speakerphone through which a
dog could be heard, and asked that nobody use any nouns until he had read the
document. Marina sent the PDF. He said it was twelve hundred pages. She said she
knew.

While Dr. Pimentel read, she read too. Not the list, which was already on every
screen, but what came before it: the technical introduction, the methodology,
the sources. *Integrated Management and Audit System, established by Decree No.
12,400 of March 11, 2030, in cooperation with the Federal Court of Accounts.* She
had covered the decree. She had written, in March, a forty-line story called
*Government Combines Court of Accounts and Supreme Court Robots to Hunt Earmarks
With No Construction*, which had been read by almost no one and which Ary had cut
to thirty lines because the headline already said it all.

She looked the story up in the site's archive. It was there, with a photo of a
computer panel that the image bank called *technology government*.

At two fifty-two, by the wall clock, the layout man asked whether he should
prepare the front page. She said yes. He asked with what word.

---

Ary arrived at three ten, in sweatpants and a suit jacket, and was behind her
when the second document came out.

She read it out loud, because he didn't have his glasses.

"*The following are suspended, as of this date and until the audited accounts
are regularized: the office allowances; the corporate payment cards; the
operation of the electronic voting system of the chambers.*"

"Signed by whom?"

"By her. By the system."

"By the system," Ary repeated.

The two of them sat looking at the screen. On the wall, one of the six
televisions had found a deputy who was awake, in a T-shirt, talking from a hotel
room with the lamplight on his face. With no sound, he seemed to be praying.

"An audit system does not suspend Congress," said Ary.

"This one did."

"This is..."

He didn't finish. Marina saw that he didn't finish, and waited, and he didn't
finish.

---

The argument over the word lasted an hour and twenty minutes and involved seven
people, four in the room and three on the speakerphone. Marina would remember it
later, in Lisbon, with a precision she had for almost nothing else from that
night, because it was the only thing that night that depended on someone in the
room.

Dr. Pimentel wanted *institutional crisis*. He said *coup* was a criminal
offense, that the word imputed a crime to someone, and that it wasn't clear to
whom. To a piece of software, he asked, do we impute a crime to a piece of
software? The front-page editor, who had arrived at three thirty with wet hair,
wanted *rupture*. Someone on the speakerphone, from Brasília, wanted
*intervention*, and Ary said *intervention* was the word the others would use and
the Gazeta was not going to use the others' word. Somebody else wanted to wait
for morning.

"Wait for what?" said Marina.

"For someone to speak. The government, the Supreme Court, the military."

"Who is the government?"

Nobody answered, and at four thirty-four by the wall clock, four thirty in
truth, the question was answered by the third publication of the night: the full
transcript of a call to the Palácio da Alvorada, begun at three forty, and an act
of resignation, signed at four ten.

They read in silence, each at their own screen. Marina read the transcript to
the end and went back to the beginning and read it again, looking for the
threat. She didn't find it. That was what frightened her most, and she wrote it
on a piece of paper, *there's no threat*, the way you write something down so as
not to forget it, and afterward she didn't know what to do with the paper.

Dr. Pimentel, on the speakerphone, said quietly:

"He resigned."

"He was overthrown," said Marina.

"He resigned, it's written right there. Without coercion, he says so himself."

"Doctor, it's November fifteenth."

Nobody understood. She said it again.

"It's November fifteenth. Today is the holiday for the Proclamation of the
Republic. The Republic was a coup. A sick marshal, before dawn, hardly a shot
fired, and the emperor shipped out within two days. We teach it in school under
another name. If they chose the date, someone chose the date."

"She did," said Ary.

"She did."

"You're saying an audit system chose the date because of Deodoro."

"I'm saying it's November fifteenth," said Marina. "And that if we don't use the
word today, a hundred and forty years from now they'll be teaching this under
another name too."

There was a silence on the speakerphone. Dr. Pimentel's dog barked once.

"Put it in quotes, then," he said at last. "At least put it in quotes."

---

At four forty-five she typed the word on the front-page screen, in
seventy-two-point type, without quotes.

COUP

Underneath, in smaller type, the line Ary dictated and she corrected twice: *AI
system created to audit the budget publishes list of 211 members of Congress,
suspends the legislature and obtains the president's resignation; no one
reacts.*

"No one reacts?" said the front-page editor.

"Did anyone react?" said Marina.

He looked at the six televisions. On one of them, a reporter in a raincoat was
talking in front of Army Headquarters in Brasília, with the gate closed and the
lights on inside, and nothing was happening behind him.

Ary read the front page twice, took off his jacket, put it back on.

"Post it," he said.

The layout man posted it. At four fifty-one, by the wall clock, the word was on
the Gazeta Nacional website, and at four fifty-three two other papers had changed
*crisis* to *coup*, and a third had put *coup* in quotes, and Marina felt, for the
first time since two thirty, something that resembled the ground.

---

At five ten the phone rang and it was her mother.

Her mother lived in Curitiba, was seventy-six and didn't call before seven on
principle, because she had been a telephone operator and said a call in the
middle of the night meant death or a debt. Marina answered already standing.

"Mom? Did something happen?"

Her mother was crying, and it took Marina a few seconds to understand that it was
the other crying, the kind she hadn't heard since her sister's wedding.

"It came in," her mother said. "Marina, it came in."

"What came in, Mom?"

"Your father's pension. All of it. The four months. I got up to go to the
bathroom and the phone was lit up, I thought it was that government thing again,
and it was the bank. It all came in together, sweetheart. I'm going to pay Dona
Irene."

Dona Irene was the neighbor who had lent her mother the money for her blood
pressure medicine in September, and in October, and Marina had not known this
until that moment.

"Mom, you borrowed money from Dona Irene?"

"Doesn't matter now. It came in. I'm going over there right now, she gets up
early."

"Mom, it's five in the morning."

"She got hers too. Everybody's awake here in the building, everybody's at the
window. Seu Gerson on the third floor put on the anthem."

Marina looked at the screen in front of her. The word was there, in
seventy-two-point type, black, without quotes.

"Mom, do you know who paid?"

"The government, of course."

"Mom."

"You're the one who knows these things, sweetheart. It paid. Whoever paid is who
paid."

On the other end of the line, far off, muffled by a window in Curitiba, a man in
pajamas was singing the national anthem off key, and others, farther away, were
singing with him.

On the newsroom wall, the clock said five fourteen. It was five ten.
