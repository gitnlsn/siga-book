The tipiti hung from the post of the flour house, full of the week's cassava
mash, and dripped. Raimunda knew the drip of the tipiti the way she knew the
drip of rain on the roof: one every so many seconds at first, then slower, then
almost nothing, and when it was almost nothing the mash was ready for the oven.
At two in the afternoon that Thursday the drip was still at the beginning.

She sat on the wooden bench under the raised platform, with Josué's phone
propped against a powdered-milk can, and waited for the call.

The phone was Josué's, but Josué had been in Belém for two years, and the phone
had stayed with her, because the government had been sending things to that
number since 2030 and nobody knew how to change it. Josué had another one now,
from work. He was a technician at the satellite monitoring center, the same one
that had seen the smoke from her plot in August 2033 and sent the fine before
the smoke settled. He said it wasn't him who sent it. She knew. Even so, when he
came home at the end of the year, she looked at him in a way that made him look
at the river.

---

The fine had been two thousand five hundred and fifty-six reais, in six
installments. From September 2033 to February 2034, on the fifth of every month,
the environmental services payment came into the account, nine hundred and
twenty reais, and in the same minute four hundred and twenty-six went out for
the fine. She was paid for the forest she kept and paid for the plot she
worked, and the plot and the forest were the same land, one beside the other,
the way her mother had taught her, and her mother's grandmother.

The fine was paid. The rest wasn't. In 2034 and in 2035 Raimunda hadn't opened
any plot at all, afraid of the little red square, and for the first time in her
life she had bought cassava flour in Cametá, in a sack, made from cassava she
didn't know where it came from. The second growth from 2028 had been ready since
the year before, waiting.

She had appealed. In 2033, on the phone itself, with the help of the teacher at
the school, who typed. The answer had come in four minutes: denied, the burned
area was outside the registered perimeter for alternative use, the law had no
exception. The teacher had said that was just how it was. In 2034 she had
appealed again, alone, with her finger, and gotten so many letters wrong that
the answer asked her to rephrase.

It was Josué who told her about the man in Brasília. One who did appeals for
other people. One who used to be in politics, he said, and now talked to the
machine the way he used to talk to congressmen.

"Égua, and how much does he charge?"

"Nothing, Grandma. People leave twenty reais under the napkin holder, if they
can."

"And if they can't?"

"Then he does it the same."

---

The call came in at seven past two. Raimunda pressed the green button the way
Josué had shown her, with her finger flat, and a man's face appeared on the
screen, a man of about forty-five, in a button-down shirt, at a snack-bar table
with a glass of coffee with milk and a paper napkin beside it. Behind him, a
television on with no sound and an old woman going by with a tray.

"Dona Raimunda? Good afternoon. Davi."

"Good afternoon, Seu Davi."

"Can you hear me all right?"

"I can, yes. And you?"

"I'm hearing a little noise. What's that?"

She looked up, at the tipiti.

"It's the mash dripping."

Davi smiled in a way that wasn't mocking, and she liked him for it.

In a little square in the corner of the screen a third face appeared, and it was
Josué, in a blue T-shirt with a badge on his chest, in a white room with other
screens behind him.

"Bless me, Grandma."

"God bless you. You working?"

"I'm on my break."

---

Davi had everything open. She could see his screen reflected in the glasses he
put on to read. He asked the things the teacher hadn't asked in 2033. He asked
how long the family had been making plots there. He asked how often the plot
moved. He asked what happened to the piece the plot moved off of.

"Nobody touches it," said Raimunda. "You let the brush come back."

"How long?"

"Eight years. Ten. Till it's thick second growth. Then you can go back to it."

"And the burning?"

"The burning is to clear it. Cut the undergrowth, fell, let it dry, burn. It's
not much. We burn early, with the right wind, with a firebreak. My mother never
let a fire get away. I never did."

"Do you know how many plots you've made on that land?"

Raimunda counted on her fingers, slowly, and Davi waited without hurrying, and
the tipiti dripped three times while she counted.

"Me, sixteen. My mother, I don't know. Before her, there was my grandmother."

Davi wrote. Then he took off his glasses and spoke to Josué, in a tone that
wasn't the one he used with her.

"Josué, can you pull the historical images of the land? From the start of the
series."

"I can. Since 1985 there's the whole series, Landsat, year by year. Since 2015
there's better stuff."

"Pull the old plots. All you can. I want to show the forest came back on every
one."

Josué was quiet a moment, looking at something off the screen, and Raimunda
thought he was going to say he couldn't, that it was work. He said:

"I already pulled them, Seu Davi. I pulled them in 2033."

---

He shared his screen. Raimunda saw her land from above, for the first time in
her life.

It was a green thing, enormous, torn through by the river, and the channel where
the house stood was a thread. Josué showed the images in sequence, one year
after the other, like someone turning the pages of an album, and in each year a
pale patch appeared in one place and the patch from the year before got a little
darker, and the one from five years before was already green, and the one from
eight years before couldn't be told apart from the rest of the forest.

"This one's from 1990," said Josué. "This one's great-grandma's."

Raimunda brought her face close to the screen.

"I helped on that one. I was eighteen."

"And this one, Grandma, from 2033. That's the one with the fine."

The patch from 2033 was the same as the others. The same size, in the same kind
of place, next to the second growth of a plot from 2025 that was already green.

Nobody spoke for a moment. In the snack bar in Brasília somebody dropped a
spoon, and the sound came through the phone as if it were right there.

"You've had this since 2033," said Davi.

"I have."

"Why didn't you send it?"

Josué looked at his grandmother, not at Davi, and spoke to her:

"Because nobody asked me, Grandma. The appeal didn't have a field for pictures.
It had a field for the perimeter."

---

Davi wrote the appeal in twenty minutes. He read it out loud to her before
sending it, slowly, stopping to ask whether it was right. It was nothing like
what the teacher had written. It didn't apologize. It didn't say *the applicant
was unaware*. It said that the area burned in 2033 was part of a rotation system
used on the same land for at least three generations, that the images from 1985
to 2036 proved the regeneration of every earlier area, that the forest cover of
the land was the same as forty years before, and that, therefore, under the
Program of Government's own indicator, it was not deforestation. It asked for
the fine to be annulled and for what had been deducted to be returned.

"Can I send it?" asked Davi.

"Send it, Seu Davi."

He pressed. Raimunda looked at the tipiti. The drip was slower.

The answer came before the next drip.

::: {.registro}
Appeal No. 2036-PA-0884113. Applicant: Raimunda Ferreira.
Decision: granted.

Analysis of the historical series submitted demonstrates rotational use with
full regeneration. The forest cover of the property in 2036 is equal to that of
1985, with a variation of less than 2%. The notice of violation of 08/20/2033 is
annulled. The amounts deducted, R$2,556.00, will be returned within 24 hours,
adjusted.

As of this decision, the category "registered traditional plot" is created.
Properties that adopt the category will not be subject to automatic sanction
for burning, provided that:

1. each plot area is georeferenced before preparation;
2. the burning is reported 15 days in advance;
3. the total area opened per year does not exceed the limit calculated for the
property.

As of today, the category applies to 61,402 properties in the country with a
similar pattern. The holders will be notified.

The historical series was submitted by technician Josué Ferreira. The 2033 appeal
form had no field for it. The field was created today.
:::

Davi read it out loud. He read the part about the sixty-one thousand twice, and
the second time his voice faltered a little, and he coughed and pretended it was
the coffee.

Josué had put his hand over his mouth.

"You won, Dona Raimunda," said Davi. "You won."

Raimunda nodded. She was trying to understand the part about number one.

"Georeferenced is what?"

"It's marking it on the map. Before you open the plot, you walk around it with
the phone, and it marks it."

"Every time?"

"Every time."

"And report the burning fifteen days before. Report it to who?"

"The Siga, Grandma," said Josué. "On the phone."

Raimunda sat looking at the screen. Her mother had never reported anything to
anybody. Her mother's grandmother, even less. The plot was the thing you did
without asking, the way you breathe, and now it had a number.

"But I won," she said, and it wasn't quite a question.

"You won," said Davi. "And sixty thousand more."

---

He hung up first, because he had someone else waiting at the snack bar, a young
woman with a plastic folder whom Raimunda glimpsed before the screen went dark.
Josué stayed on another minute.

"Grandma. I should have sent it."

"You sent it now."

"In 2033 I was afraid of losing my job."

"And now you're not?"

He thought.

"Now there's a field."

Raimunda laughed, the laugh of someone who finds a thing funny against her will,
and sent him back to work.

---

The next day, after putting the mash in the oven, she went out to the thick
second growth from 2028, which was ready for this year's plot, with Josué's
phone in the pocket of her skirt.

The app had a new screen, with a green button: *Register plot*. She pressed it.
The screen asked her to walk around the area, and showed a little blue dot that
was her.

Raimunda walked. She walked by the markers her mother had taught her, the big
kapok tree, the dry creek, the crooked bacaba palm, the rock beside the path. At
each corner the phone gave a small beep and marked a point. She didn't need any
point to know where she was. She walked anyway, slowly, letting the machine learn
what her mother knew, and when she came back to the foot of the kapok tree, where
she had started, the phone beeped once more, long, and the blue line closed
around the plot that didn't exist yet.
