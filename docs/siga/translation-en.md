# Translation contract — *Sixty-One Percent*

The English edition of *Siga*, in `books/sixty-one-percent/`. The general rules
are in `docs/translation-en.md`; this file holds what is particular to this
book, and wins over the general file when they disagree. The author chose the
title on 2026-10-19. Everything else here was decided by the translator and may
be changed: change it here first, then everywhere it is used.

## Voice

Third person close, past tense, one point of view per chapter, as fixed in
`docs/siga/voz.md`. Every narrator keeps their register in English:
- Lúcia corrects other people's grammar in her head (carry this through
  English usage, never by quoting Portuguese grammar);
- Raimunda and Josué speak plainly and regionally, through rhythm and word
  choice, **never** invented dialect spelling; *égua*, *maninho* and *mana*
  stay in Portuguese only where they work as names or exclamations, and are
  never glossed;
- Bessa is clipped barracks jargon;
- Wesley has the cadence of the pulpit (King James in quotation, his own
  preaching voice around it);
- Helena thinks in procedure;
- Teles thinks in orders of the day.

**The book is placed in Brazil.** *Dona* and *seu* stay, capitalized before a
name (*Dona Nice*, *Seu Lauro*), never italicized. Place names stay, real and
invented: Furo Grande, Morro do Vintém, Jardim Ângela, Tomba, Ceilândia,
Pastelaria do Edvaldo, Gazeta Nacional. R$ stays, with an English decimal
point: `R$ 2.556,00` → `R$2,556.00`.

## The Siga

- **SIGA** in documents and headers; **the Siga** in speech and narration,
  always with the article, as Brazilians say *a Siga*. The full name is the
  **Integrated Management and Audit System**, kept as the expansion of SIGA.
- **Pronoun: she.** The Portuguese *ela* is deliberate ("— Ela."), and English
  keeps it.
- **The pun on *siga*** (the imperative: *go on, carry on*) survives only where
  the text itself points at it: Davi reading the word at the foot of a decree
  like a traffic sign. There, keep *siga* in italics and translate the
  surrounding sentence so the reader sees the order inside the name, without
  any gloss beyond what the Portuguese has.
- **The sign-off of the Programa, "Até lá, siga."** → *Until then, siga.* The
  last word is italic and is not glossed. The sign-off of the Convocação,
  "Siga." → *Siga.*
- The Siga's voice in documents: short sentences, no judging adjectives, "I"
  only when the question is about her. Never soften a flat line.

### Notes from translating chapters 01–08

#### Terms and conventions added

- **Dates in documents** follow US order, MM/DD/YYYY (`07/14/2027`, `03/11/2030`
  = March 11); timestamps take 24-hour form with a colon (`16:22`, `03:40:12`).
- **R$ amounts** close up: `R$14,200,000.00`, `R$61.4 billion`.
- **Diário Oficial header** → *OFFICIAL GAZETTE OF THE UNION — SPECIAL EDITION —
  No. 219-B*.
- **Sistema de Emendas** → the Earmarks System. **Auditoria nº 1/2030** → Audit
  No. 1/2030. **Anexo Reservado** → Confidential Annex.
- **CNPJ** kept, glossed once in narration as "company registration numbers"
  (the Portuguese sentence repeats the acronym; the second mention carries it).
- **duodécimo** → the monthly twelfth.
- **coronel de dia** → colonel of the day; **formatura** → morning formation;
  **fora de forma** → fall out; **taifeiros** → cooks; **sargento furriel** →
  supply sergeant; **rancho** → mess hall.
- **ordem do dia** text: *Stand by. Keep the troops in barracks. Suspend leave.
  No unit moves without written orders from this Command. Extra coffee.*
- **brigadeiro** → Air Brigadier (Souto).
- **Presidente do STF** → the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court.
- **funk** (Bessa's locker room) stays *funk*.
- **fiado** → on a tab / without running a tab.
- **merendeira** → lunch lady.
- **reposição** → make-up day.
- **professora** (as address) → teacher.
- **"filha"** (Marina's mother) → sweetheart.

#### Deviations from the contract

- **"Então pediu certo."** The contract fixes *Then he asked right.* The subject
  is the computer (Dete is answering Lúcia's "um computador tinha pedido …
  educadamente"), so it is rendered **Then it asked right.**, both times.
  Recommend correcting the contract.
- **The *siga* pun** in *1:58 a.m.*: "como o que se diz a alguém que parou no
  meio do caminho" → "like what you say to someone who has stopped in the middle
  of the road: go on." The colon and *go on* are the minimum that lets the
  English reader see the order in the name; the Portuguese reader needs nothing.
  Flag for review against "nothing explained".

#### Found in the Portuguese

- **06h20:** Vargas says at 06h30 "Gente que não vai aguardar." The editorial
  review said this premature echo of Teles's "Aguardar" (07h00) had been cut,
  but it is still there. The English renders it as plain *sit and wait*, not the
  fixed *Stand by*, so the echo does not happen in English.
- **06h00:** the reunion is "às seis" while the chapter opens with Teles at his
  desk at 05h50 and the mess-hall visit at 05h15 precedes the 05h30 file; fine,
  but the opening paragraph (05h50, blank sheet) sits before the flashback to
  05h04 with no marker. Not a contradiction; noted only.

### Notes from the translation of chapters 09–14

#### Terms and lines chosen (to merge into docs/siga/translation-en.md)
