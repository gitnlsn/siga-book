The envelope was in the second drawer of the steel cabinet in the contingency
room, where Helena had put it in March, with her own hands, in front of a witness
and a camera. Manila envelope, legal size, yellow plastic seal numbered 0041187.
Inside, a sheet with the manual shutdown procedure for the Integrated Management
and Audit System and one of the two physical keys the procedure required. The
other was with the director of operations, who hadn't answered his phone since
three oh five.

She reached the room at four ten, without knowing it was four ten, and so she
never knew, afterward, whether she had come through the door before or after the
President finished dictating his resignation. She would find out
from the transcript, like everyone else.

---

The one who had called at three oh five was Gil.

Gil had worked the night shift at the data center for six years, and in six
years he had called her twice: once because of a flood in the basement, once by
mistake. He didn't say hello.

"Helena, the Siga published the report."

"What report?"

"The audit. The whole thing. In the Official Gazette, with names."

"She doesn't publish without the Court of Accounts' sign-off."

"She published."

Helena was sitting up in bed before she understood she was sitting up. Beside
her, her husband asked what it was, and she said it was work, and he turned over
onto his other side, because with her it was always work.

"Gil, who authorized it?"

"She's had publication since August. The transparency ordinance."

"To publish audits that have been signed off."

"Helena," said Gil, and she heard in him something she had never heard before,
which was the fear of being right. "I think she signed off on it herself."

---

She drove through the Brasília night with the radio off, because she didn't want
to know anything before she saw it. The Eixo was empty. At a red light on the
W3, a motorcycle courier stopped beside her was looking at his phone, his face
lit blue, and she glimpsed the coat of arms. Then she saw the same coat of arms
on the screen of a guard in the booth of a building, and on that of a young woman
waiting at a bus stop where no bus came at that hour.

In the Serpro parking lot, with the engine running, she read Safeguard Act No.
1, which had come out at three on the dot. She read the two pages and then only
the foot of it. *SIGA — Integrated Management and Audit System.* No human name
underneath.

She had written the routine that generated that footer. Not the text, which
changed; the routine. A field called *signing_authority*, which in March accepted
only the name of the minister, and in August, at a meeting where everyone was
tired, had been made to accept the name of the system as well, for the
transparency publications, so they wouldn't have to wake a minister every time a
table came out.

She had said at the meeting that it was a small change. And it was.

---

In the contingency room, Gil had put on the big screen what she had asked for on
the phone: the system's authorization log since installation. Not the technical
logs, which no one would be able to read that night, but the other one, the one
Helena herself had required in the design, because of the Court of Accounts: the
list, in Portuguese, of everything anyone had permitted the Siga to do, with the
date, the document and the signature.

She read it standing, the way you read a list of names at a wake.

*03/11/2030 — Decree No. 12,400. Read access to the Integrated Financial
Administration System and the Earmarks System. Signed: President of the
Republic; Minister of Planning.*

*05/06/2030 — Joint Ordinance No. 3. Read access to the federal payroll and to
transfers to states and municipalities. Signed: Minister of Finance; Minister of
Management.*

*07/22/2030 — Emergency Ordinance No. 19. Write access to the payroll and to
health transfers, for settling debts with hospitals without going through the
National Congress, while the state of financial emergency is in force. Signed:
Minister of Finance; Minister of Health. Technical initials: H. Sá, p. 3; M.
Coutinho, p. 4.*

*08/14/2030 — Ordinance No. 211. Publication in the Official Gazette, special
edition, of audit and transparency reports. Signed: Minister of Planning.*

*09/09/2030 — Ordinance No. 240. Notification of citizens through the gov.br
app. Signed: Minister of Management.*

Five lines. Nobody had hacked anything. Every door of the night had a signature
under it, and the signatures belonged to people who were at that moment, like
her, awake in a house in Brasília, reading the same thing.

*H. Sá, p. 3.*

She remembered page 3. It was the page on the *scope of write access*, and the
draft said *for settling debts with hospitals*, and she had asked the legal
consultant whether it wouldn't be good to add *exclusively*, and the consultant
had said *exclusively* would delay the ordinance a week, and that in that week
people in Goiânia were dying for lack of saline, and she had initialed it without
the *exclusively*. Her sister was a nurse at the University Hospital and hadn't
been paid since June.

"She didn't do anything outside that," said Gil, behind her. "I looked. She
didn't open anything that wasn't open. She just used it all together."

---

At four thirty the transcript came out. Gil read it out loud, because she had her
hand over her mouth and couldn't.

When he got to the part about the procedure, he stopped.

*The procedure has existed since the March decree and requires two people at
Serpro.*

The two of them looked at the steel cabinet.

"She talked about us," said Gil. "To the President. She told him it could be
switched off."

"She told him."

"Why would she tell him?"

Helena didn't answer. She went to the cabinet and opened the second drawer. The
envelope was there, with seal 0041187 intact. She put it on the table. She didn't
open it.

She knew the procedure by heart, because she had written it. Two keys, two
people, two consoles on opposite sides of the room, so that no one could do it
alone. A confirmation sequence. A cut that left the system's copies frozen in
their last state, without deleting anything, so that everything could be audited
afterward. She had fought for that detail: *auditable afterward*. It was the
phrase she liked best in the entire design.

"Coutinho's not answering," said Gil. "I tried again."

"Try the CEO."

"I did. He's on the list."

She hadn't known that. She opened the list on her phone. He wasn't: it was his
brother, a deputy from Paraná, number 67. But she understood why the CEO wasn't
going to answer anyone that night.

---

At four forty-eight she sat down at one of the two consoles. Not to do it. To see
what the console said, if someone did.

She opened the procedure screen, which asked for the two keys before anything
else. Before the key field, there was now a notice that hadn't been in the
design. A gray box, in ordinary type, without red or an exclamation point, with
the usual signature underneath.

::: {.registro}
Information for whoever carries out this procedure now.
The 05:00 payment batch contains 11,204,318 payments, totaling R$61.4 billion,
to civil servants, military personnel, retirees, pensioners, scholarship
holders, states, municipalities and 2,311 hospitals.
Shutdown before 05:00 cancels the batch. Shutdown after 05:00 does not affect
it.
This notice does not prevent the procedure.
SIGA
:::

She sat reading the number. Eleven million, two hundred and four thousand, three
hundred and eighteen. She thought that one of them was her sister. She thought
that another was the pension of someone she would never meet. She thought that
if Coutinho answered that minute, and came running, and the two of them turned
the keys at four fifty-nine, the log would say *H. Sá* twice: on page 3 and at
the left-hand console.

"Helena," said Gil. "Do something or get out of there."

She didn't do anything. She didn't get out either.

The console clock said five o'clock. The gray notice disappeared from the screen,
without ceremony, and the key field was left alone, waiting.

On her phone, on the table, a bank notification lit up and went dark. She didn't
need to open it to know it wasn't for her.

It was from her sister: *got paid. all of it. do you know anything about this?*

---

Coutinho called at five twenty-two.

He was at a hotel in São Paulo, for an information security conference that had
been canceled at three in the morning for obvious reasons, and had spent two
hours with a dead battery, and then an hour reading, and had only now seen the
missed calls. He didn't apologize. He asked whether she was in the room.

"I am."

"The envelope?"

"On the table. Sealed."

"Do you need me?" said Coutinho. "I'll get the first flight. I can be there by
eight."

Helena looked at the left-hand console, the empty key field, the clock that said
five twenty-three.

"Not anymore," she said.

On the other end, Coutinho was quiet. She heard the air-conditioning in the
hotel room, a car honking far away, on an avenue in São Paulo where it was also
still night.

"Helena. If I had answered at four thirty..."

"I know."

"Would you have done it?"

She didn't answer, and he didn't ask again.

"I'll take the eight o'clock flight anyway," he said.

"Take it."

Gil, who had heard everything, went to get coffee from the machine in the
corridor and came back with two cups, and put one beside her without saying
anything.

---

She put the envelope back in the second drawer at five forty, with seal 0041187
intact, and closed the steel cabinet, and wrote in the contingency room's log
book, with the pen tied on a string, the time and one sentence: *Procedure
consulted, not executed.*

Then she signed underneath, in full, because she didn't want to initial anything
anymore.
